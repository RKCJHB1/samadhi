# Ranti Deva

King Ranti Deva was a great ruler who became famous not for his wealth or power, but for his extraordinary compassion and selfless service to others. His story teaches us about seeing God in all beings and serving others as the highest form of worship.

## The Compassionate King

Ranti Deva ruled a prosperous kingdom, but what made him special was his incredible kindness.

### A Heart Full of Love
Unlike many kings who focused on power and wealth, Ranti Deva's heart was filled with love for all beings.

He saw every person, rich or poor, as a manifestation of God deserving respect and care.

### Open Doors Policy
His palace doors were always open to anyone in need.

No one was ever turned away hungry or without help from his kingdom.

## The Daily Routine of Service

Every day, King Ranti Deva would serve thousands of people.

### Feeding the Hungry
Before eating his own meals, the king would ensure that everyone in his kingdom had been fed.

He considered it his sacred duty to make sure no one went hungry.

### Caring for All
His compassion extended to:
- The poor and homeless
- Travelers and pilgrims
- Animals and birds
- Anyone in distress

### Personal Service
The king didn't just order others to help; he personally served those in need.

He would wash the feet of guests and serve food with his own hands.

## The Great Test

One day, King Ranti Deva faced the greatest test of his compassion and faith.

### The Long Fast
Due to various circumstances, the king had been fasting for 48 days.

Finally, food was prepared for him to break his fast.

### The First Visitor
Just as he was about to eat, a hungry Brahmin (priest) came to his door.

Without hesitation, Ranti Deva gave half his food to the visitor.

### The Second Visitor
As he was about to eat the remaining food, a poor farmer arrived, weak with hunger.

The king gave him most of the remaining food, keeping only a little for himself.

### The Third Visitor
Before he could take even a bite, a tribal man came with his dogs, all starving.

Ranti Deva gave them the rest of his solid food, keeping only some water.

### The Final Test
Just as he raised the water to his lips, an untouchable (considered lowest in society) came begging for water.

Without any hesitation, the king gave away his last bit of water.

## The Divine Revelation

As soon as Ranti Deva gave away the water, something miraculous happened.

### The Visitors' True Identity
The four visitors revealed themselves to be Lord Vishnu, Lord Shiva, Lord Brahma, and Lord Indra in disguise.

They had come to test the king's devotion and compassion.

### Divine Appreciation
The gods were amazed by Ranti Deva's selfless love and complete surrender.

Even when facing death from hunger and thirst, he had put others' needs before his own.

### The Ultimate Blessing
The gods offered to grant him any wish he desired.

They were prepared to give him anything - immortality, unlimited wealth, or divine powers.

## Ranti Deva's Remarkable Response

The king's response to the gods' offer revealed the true greatness of his character.

### No Personal Desires
"I don't want anything for myself," said Ranti Deva.

He had no desire for personal gain, even when offered divine blessings.

### His Only Wish
"If you must grant me something, let me always see you in every being I meet."

The king wanted only the ability to serve God by serving others.

### The Perfect Prayer
"Let me never forget that you dwell in every heart, and let me serve you through serving all beings."

This prayer showed his understanding that serving others is the highest form of worship.

## The Deeper Teaching

Ranti Deva's story contains profound spiritual lessons.

### God in Everyone
The king understood that God lives in every being, regardless of their social status.

He served the untouchable with the same love as he served the Brahmin.

### True Worship
Real devotion to God is not just prayer and ritual, but serving those in need.

When we help others, we are directly serving God.

### Selfless Love
The highest form of love is that which expects nothing in return.

Ranti Deva served others without any thought of reward or recognition.

### Equality of All Beings
The king treated everyone equally, seeing past external differences to the divine soul within.

## Lessons for Our Lives

### Seeing God in Others
We can practice seeing the divine presence in everyone we meet.

This changes how we treat people and makes us more compassionate.

### Service as Worship
We can make our daily acts of helping others into forms of spiritual practice.

Serving family, friends, and community becomes a way of worshipping God.

### Putting Others First
Like Ranti Deva, we can learn to consider others' needs alongside our own.

This doesn't mean neglecting ourselves, but being generous and caring.

### Breaking Down Barriers
We can work to overcome prejudices and treat all people with equal respect.

Social status, wealth, or background shouldn't determine how we treat others.

## Modern Applications

### In Family Life
- Helping family members without expecting thanks
- Sharing resources generously
- Caring for elderly relatives
- Teaching children to be helpful

### In Community
- Volunteering for charitable causes
- Helping neighbors in need
- Supporting food banks and shelters
- Being kind to service workers

### In Daily Interactions
- Treating everyone with respect
- Helping strangers when possible
- Being patient with difficult people
- Seeing the good in everyone

## The King's Legacy

### Inspiration for Rulers
Ranti Deva became the ideal of what a ruler should be - a servant of the people.

His example inspired many kings to govern with compassion and justice.

### Model for All
His story shows that anyone, regardless of position, can serve others and find God.

We don't need to be kings to practice his teachings.

### Timeless Relevance
In our modern world, his message of service and compassion is more needed than ever.

His example can guide us in creating a more caring society.

## The Ultimate Realization

### True Wealth
Ranti Deva discovered that the greatest wealth is the ability to serve others.

Material possessions are temporary, but the joy of service is eternal.

### Real Power
True power lies not in controlling others, but in the ability to help and heal.

The king's influence came from his love, not his authority.

### Divine Vision
By serving all beings equally, Ranti Deva achieved the highest spiritual vision.

He saw God everywhere and in everyone.

## Conclusion

King Ranti Deva's story teaches us that the highest form of spirituality is selfless service to others. By seeing God in every being and serving them with love, we can transform our daily lives into continuous worship. His example shows us that true greatness lies not in what we accumulate for ourselves, but in what we give to others. Through compassion, service, and the vision of divine presence in all beings, we can follow in the footsteps of this great king and find the deepest meaning and joy in life.

## Key Terms

- **Selfless Service**: Helping others without expecting anything in return
- **Divine Vision**: The ability to see God's presence in all beings
- **Compassionate Leadership**: Ruling or leading with love and care for all
- **Service as Worship**: Understanding that helping others is a form of devotion to God
