# The Hare and Donkey

This wise story teaches us about the dangers of pride and the importance of humility, showing us that true worth comes from our character, not our appearance or abilities.

## The Proud Hare

In a forest, there lived a hare who was very proud of his speed and agility.

### Boasting About Speed
The hare would often boast to other animals about how fast he could run.

"Look at me!" he would say. "I am the fastest animal in this forest!"

### Looking Down on Others
Because of his speed, the hare began to look down on other animals, especially those who were slower than him.

He thought that being fast made him better and more important than everyone else.

## The Humble Donkey

In the same forest lived a donkey who was known for being slow but very hardworking.

### Quiet and Hardworking
The donkey never boasted about anything and quietly went about his daily work.

He helped carry heavy loads for the villagers and was always willing to help others.

### Patient and Kind
Unlike the hare, the donkey was patient with other animals and never made fun of anyone.

He treated everyone with kindness and respect, regardless of their abilities.

## The Hare's Mockery

One day, the hare saw the donkey carrying a heavy load and decided to make fun of him.

### Cruel Words
"Look at that slow, stupid donkey!" laughed the hare. "He's so slow and clumsy!"

The hare ran circles around the donkey, showing off his speed and making the donkey feel embarrassed.

### Hurting Feelings
The donkey felt hurt by the hare's cruel words but didn't say anything back.

He continued his work quietly, even though his feelings were wounded.

## The Day of the Flood

One day, heavy rains came to the forest, and the river began to flood.

### Rising Waters
The water rose quickly, and all the animals were in danger.

They needed to reach higher ground to save themselves from the flood.

### Animals in Panic
All the forest animals were running toward the hills, trying to escape the rising water.

The situation was becoming very dangerous for everyone.

## The Hare's Problem

When the flood came, the hare discovered that his speed wasn't enough to save him.

### Unable to Cross
The hare reached the flooded river but couldn't cross it because the current was too strong.

His speed was useless when he needed to cross the deep, rushing water.

### Stuck and Scared
The hare was stuck on the wrong side of the river, with the water rising around him.

He was scared and didn't know what to do.

## The Donkey's Help

The donkey, despite being mocked by the hare, came to his rescue.

### Offering Help
"Jump on my back," said the donkey kindly. "I can carry you across the river safely."

The donkey was strong enough to walk through the deep water and carry the hare to safety.

### No Grudges
Even though the hare had been cruel to him, the donkey didn't hold a grudge.

He was willing to help because it was the right thing to do.

## Crossing the River

The donkey carefully carried the hare across the dangerous, flooded river.

### Steady and Strong
While the hare's speed was useless in the water, the donkey's strength and steadiness saved both their lives.

The donkey's slow, careful steps were exactly what was needed in this situation.

### Safe on the Other Side
Thanks to the donkey's help, both animals reached safety on the other side of the river.

The hare's life had been saved by the very animal he had mocked.

## The Hare's Lesson

After being saved by the donkey, the hare learned an important lesson about humility.

### Feeling Ashamed
The hare felt deeply ashamed of how he had treated the donkey.

He realized that his pride and cruelty had been wrong.

### Asking for Forgiveness
"I'm sorry for making fun of you," said the hare. "You saved my life even though I was mean to you."

The hare learned that true greatness comes from kindness, not from being fast or clever.

## The Donkey's Wisdom

The donkey's response showed his wisdom and good character.

### Forgiving Heart
"It's okay," said the donkey gently. "We all have different gifts, and we should use them to help each other."

The donkey didn't hold the hare's past behavior against him.

### Teaching Moment
"Your speed is a wonderful gift," the donkey continued. "But my strength is useful too. We all have something valuable to offer."

## The Lesson for Us

This story teaches us several important lessons about how we should treat others and think about ourselves.

### Don't Be Proud
Having special talents or abilities should not make us proud or cause us to look down on others.

Every person has their own unique gifts and value.

### Don't Judge by Appearance
We shouldn't judge others based on how they look or what abilities they seem to have.

Sometimes the people we think are "less than us" are actually the ones who can help us most.

### Help Others Regardless
We should help others even if they haven't been kind to us in the past.

Doing the right thing is more important than getting revenge.

### Everyone Has Value
Every person has something valuable to contribute, even if it's not immediately obvious.

We should respect and appreciate the different gifts that people have.

## The Spiritual Message

This story has deep spiritual meaning about how God sees us and how we should see others.

### God Values Character
God doesn't judge us by our external abilities but by our character and how we treat others.

Kindness and humility are more valuable than speed, beauty, or cleverness.

### Serving Others
True spiritual greatness comes from serving others, especially those who cannot help us in return.

### Divine Justice
God often uses the humble and overlooked people to accomplish important things.

## Conclusion

The story of the hare and donkey reminds us that pride goes before a fall, while humility and kindness lead to true greatness. The hare's speed, which he was so proud of, couldn't save him when he really needed help. But the donkey's strength and kind heart, which the hare had mocked, became the very things that saved both their lives. This teaches us to be humble about our own abilities, to respect others regardless of their apparent limitations, and to always be ready to help those in need, even if they haven't been kind to us.

## Key Terms

- **Pride**: Thinking too highly of oneself and looking down on others
- **Humility**: Being modest about one's abilities and treating others with respect
- **True Worth**: Value that comes from character and kindness, not external abilities
- **Forgiveness**: Letting go of hurt feelings and helping others despite past wrongs
